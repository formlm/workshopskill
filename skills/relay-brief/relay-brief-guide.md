# Relay Brief — Facilitator Guide

> Folder: `relay-brief` · Category: communication · 8–20 people (lines of 4–6) · 20 min · offline + online · print props · assessment-type
> License: CC BY 4.0

A five-constraint task brief is whispered down a line of four or six people, and the last person rebuilds it out loud. The distance between the original and the rebuild is the whole lesson: a verbal handover loses information at every hop, and everyone along the line felt certain they passed it on fine. Nobody has to be careless for this to happen — which is exactly why the exercise works on teams that blame inattentiveness.

## Objectives

By the end of the activity, participants will have:

1. Watched a five-constraint brief arrive with two or three constraints intact — **while every hop was honest**.
2. Caught themselves or a teammate filling a hole with a plausible guess instead of a question.
3. Named one written-handover habit they will use on the next real handover, from the loss they actually saw.

## When to use this activity

- A team keeps getting work wrong downstream and blames inattentiveness; the real culprit is the handover itself.
- You want a fast, visceral demo of why specs need to be written down and read back, not "cleared up" in a call.
- You have twenty minutes and a line of 4–6 people (run two lines in parallel for a bigger room).

## What one hop does

Each hop does three things to the brief: **drops** a constraint, **compresses** a number ("by Friday the 12th" becomes "soon"), and **injects** a plausible guess to fill the hole. Nobody lies — every person genuinely believes they passed it on accurately. That confident distortion is what the debrief lands on.

## Setup

**Clock:** twenty minutes on the floor, measures included. A tight room can run the relay itself in eight minutes; the reveal and the debrief are what you protect.

**Space:** a clear line of four to six people standing or seated facing away from one another, far enough that neighbouring lines cannot overhear. Two or three lines can run in parallel in a normal training room.

**Materials per line:**

| Item | Source |
|---|---|
| 1 × brief card (facilitator only — never leaves your hand) | `relay-brief-prop-cards.pdf` (the Brief) |
| 1 × rebuild-record card per person in the last three hops | `relay-brief-prop-cards.pdf` (Your Rebuild) |
| 1 × observer tally card | `relay-brief-prop-cards.pdf` (Tally) |
| 1 × rule card — *no notes, no repeats* | `relay-brief-prop-cards.pdf`, or read it aloud |
| 1 × participant workbook per person | `relay-brief-workbook.pdf` (US Letter: `relay-brief-workbook-Letter.pdf`) |
| 2 × answer sheet per person | `relay-brief-paper-form.pdf` — one for the pre, one for the post |

**Grouping rule:** put the line together from people who actually hand work to each other — a real chain, not a random six. The transfer lands harder when the line is the team.

**Roles:** person one (the only reader of the brief), the middle hops (each passes once), the last person (rebuilds aloud), one or two observers (tally card, may not speak during the relay).

## How to run it

| Time | Phase | Facilitator does | Participants do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Pre-measure | "Five statements about your own last week of handovers — not about the game." | Answer alone on the answer sheet |
| 2–3 | Line up | Form the line, facing away. State the two rules: no notes, one pass only. | Stand or sit in line; the last three hold a rebuild card |
| 3–11 | Relay | Show the brief card to person one for ten seconds, then take it back. "Pass it on, once, quietly." Move down the line with the timer. | Each hop tells the next in private; the last three write their own version before hearing the next |
| 11–14 | Reveal | "Last person: state the brief as you heard it." Then read the original aloud, slowly. Line the two up in front of the line. | The line counts surviving constraints on the tally card, hop by hop |
| 14–18 | Debrief | Run the debrief sheet with both versions visible. | Name what was dropped, compressed, invented; choose one habit |
| 18–20 | Post-measure | Same five statements, now about what the relay just showed them. | Answer alone; compare the shift, not the person |

Then run the debrief — see `relay-brief-debrief.md`.

## The brief card

Write one brief with **exactly five** deliberate constraints and show it only to person one. Each constraint must be something a line can lose. Example (swap in your own domain — keep the shape):

> "Reprint the **Q3 field guide**, **250 copies**, **coil-bound**, in **matte**, **deliver to the Nairobi office by Friday the 12th**."

The five work best as a mix: one quantity, one process, one finish, one place, one deadline. Vivid items survive; a bare date or a finish spec is what quietly falls out — the same thing that falls out of real handovers.

## Failure modes

1. **People write notes.** Rescue: hold the rule and say why — the leak is the point, and a note is a patch that hides it.
2. **One comedian breaks it on purpose.** Their joke lands, but the room learns "someone messed up" instead of "the channel leaks." Rescue: reset the line — everyone passes honestly — and take the fun into the debrief, where the pattern is the finding.
3. **The room hunts a culprit.** Rescue: read the hops in order — "hop three only had what hop two gave them." The system leaks; the person did not.
4. **A hop asks for a repeat.** Rescue: no repeats is the only rule you must never bend. One telling is what a Tuesday afternoon voicemail is.

## Variations

- **Online:** run the line as a serial chain of private messages, one hop at a time, each person forwarding from memory only, no scrolling back. The reveal is a screenshot wall.
- **Large groups (20+):** run two or three lines in parallel with a co-facilitator or an observer per line, then compare the surviving-constraint counts in plenary — different lines, same leak pattern.
- **Written-handover variant:** replace the whisper with a chain of forwards, each person writing to the next from what they received, thirty seconds per hop. The loss changes shape — the place survives, the deadline and the finish die — and the lesson about a written spec gets sharper.
- **Short version (12 min):** four-person line, one relay, no measures, six-minute debrief. Keep the reveal; without the side-by-side there is no evidence.
- **Assessment-type:** run the pre-measure before lining up and the post-measure right after the debrief — see `relay-brief-assessment.json`. The interesting number is how confidence in "one clear telling" collapses, not the score.

## Facilitation notes

- The strong moment is the first person hearing the final rebuild and realising it came from their own accurate telling.
- Read the original aloud at the reveal *slowly*, once. Rereading it softens the finding.
- Strongest transfer question: "Which handover this week went down a line like this one?"
