The handover, every four minutes

  • Restate first — the incoming lead says goal, done-so-far, next in one breath before directing anyone.
  • Make one call — a visible decision with a named owner. Leading shows up in a decision, not a title.
  • Pass it on — end the round with a clean summary for whoever takes over next.

Two rules hold the relay

  • Every handover restates goal, done-so-far and next before directing. If the new lead cannot, that gap is the lesson.
  • Each lead makes one visible, owned call. A round with no decision is a discussion, not a lead.
  • Above twelve people, run two parallel rounds of the same task.

Three ways it stalls, three rescues

  • The task is too big to move in four minutes → shrink it before you start. A task that cannot advance in four rounds is a project, and the room will flail.
  • The real manager keeps re-taking the pen → that is the finding. Note who it displaces and how the room reacts, then ask them to take a normal turn near the end.
  • Everyone just repeats the previous lead → restating is not parroting: require the new lead to name the next move as their own call.

What leaves the room

  • A finished, real output from the task — not a rehearsal.
  • A shared picture of who can lead a round cleanly, and who needs the restate prompt.
  • A written handover pattern (goal / done / next) the team keeps using when the lead genuinely rotates.

Three questions before you close

Which handovers landed cleanly, and which made the team lose a beat?

Who surprised you — someone you did not expect to hold the thread well, or badly?

Where does the lead genuinely rotate in your real work — standups, incidents, reviews?

What carries into real work

  • Rotate the least-senior person first in onboarding — the safest place to practise holding the thread.
  • Distributed team: rotate in a fixed order on a shared doc; restating before directing matters even more when nobody shares a room.
  • After the rounds, ask who felt the thread get stronger and who felt it wobble — where it wobbles is your real handover risk.
