The structure

  • Three cards per person: START, STOP, CONTINUE. One behavior per card.
  • Eight minutes silent writing. Seven minutes on the wall — reads and live clustering.
  • Six minutes dot-voting, three dots each. Six minutes: owner and date, said out loud.

The card rule — say it out loud

  • “Stop cc-ing the whole team” — not “stop cc-ing Dana.”
  • Behaviors, never people. Every time, no exceptions.
  • CONTINUE capped at three cards per person — or the wall turns to flattery.

Why the discipline is the design

  • The structure is tiny; the retro’s quality is all in what happens after the wall.
  • Top card per pile gets an owner and a date — auditable by anyone, including next week’s you.
  • The wall photo is the contract. Someone owns it; the next retro starts from it.

Three ways it fails, three rescues

  • Cards arrive pre-blamed (“stop being late — meaning Kai”) → rewrite live, with permission: name the behavior.
  • Everything lands in CONTINUE → cap it at three; scarcity moves honesty to the hard piles.
  • The vote splits into camps → silent dot-voting, then re-cluster the top five into one shortlist.

Three questions before you commit

1. Which pile is biggest — and what does the shape say about this team right now?

2. Which card was hardest to write? Why that one?

3. Read the three owner-plus-date cards aloud — do all three still feel real, right now?

The measure

  • Before the cards go out: six rows about how this team actually reviews itself.
  • After the close: the same six rows about this retro.
  • Watch the row about action items surviving — that is the retro’s whole reason.

Artifacts, not feelings

  • Between retro and retro, the three verbs show up in 1:1s and threads — uninvited. That is the goal.
