# Same Boat — Facilitator Guide

> Folder: `same-boat` · Category: conflict · 6–16 people · 20 min · offline + online · print props · tool pack
> License: CC BY 4.0

Two groups at odds each get five minutes to write down what they will **both** lose if the dispute keeps running — not what the other side is doing wrong, what you share as the cost. They read each other's lists and the overlapping items go on one "same boat" card. The move from "against them" to "beside them, facing the same problem" is the whole technique.

## Objectives

By the end of the activity, participants will have:

1. Named, in concrete terms, what both sides lose if the dispute keeps running.
2. Put those shared losses — not the other side's faults — on one card sitting between them.
3. Committed to one shared loss they will act on together this week.

## When to use this activity

- Two teams, functions or sub-groups have settled into blame and the argument loops the same three grievances.
- The conflict is real but the framing is positional — everyone can name the other side's faults and nothing of the shared cost.
- You have twenty minutes and can seat the two sides apart but in one room (or two breakout rooms that reconvene).

## Why "both lose" flips it

Blame points backward at each other; a shared loss points forward at a problem neither side wants. Naming something you will both be worse off for forces the two groups to look at the same object instead of at each other — the smallest concrete reframe from adversarial to joint.

## Setup

**Space:** seat the two sides apart but in one room (or two breakout rooms that reconvene to compare). A wall or board between them will hold the shared card.

**Materials per side:** one write card and a pen — each side lists what you **both** lose if this keeps going.

**Materials for the room:** one large "same boat" card and one "still different" card from `same-boat-prop-cards.pdf`.

**Grouping:** the two actual sides of a live dispute, 6–16 people total. Too hot to share a room? Run the writing in separate rooms and reconvene only to compare.

**Roles:** each side writes together, then reads its list aloud; you hold the "both lose only" rule and the clock.

## How to run it

| Time | Phase | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Frame | State the rule: describe only what BOTH sides will lose, no "they do X". Write it down. |
| 2–7 | A writes | Side A, apart, lists what you both lose if this keeps going — money, time, trust, a launch, a relationship. |
| 2–7 | B writes | Side B does the same at the same time, apart from A. |
| 7–14 | Compare | Each side reads its list aloud. Anything that appears on both goes on the shared "same boat" card. |
| 14–18 | Reframe | Around the shared items only: what would change if you treated that as the problem, not each other? |
| 18–20 | Bridge | Each side names one shared loss they will act on this week, together. |

## The card

Write the overlapping items on `same-boat-prop-cards.pdf`'s large "same boat" card and leave it up between the two groups. By the end it should hold three or more losses neither side can pin on the other. Use the "still different" card to hold the real disagreements you are not pretending away.

## Failure modes

- **A side writes "what they're doing wrong."** Interrupt gently; the rule is only shared losses. Re-read it before writing.
- **No genuine overlap appears.** If nothing matches, the disagreement is being described too abstractly — force concrete costs (a date, a sum, a name).
- **A win feels implied.** This is not "we agreed" — it is one object both can see. Keep it at "same boat, different oars."

## Variations

- **Two rooms:** run the writing in separate breakouts, then reconvene only to compare and build the card.
- **Remote:** each side writes in a private doc; share the two lists on one screen to find the overlap.
- **Shortened (12 min):** one round of writing plus the compare and a verbal bridge — skip the written reframe.
- **De-escalation on a live thread:** paste the two lists into the argument itself; the overlapping items often reset the tone on their own.

## Facilitation notes

- The strongest moment is when a side hears its own hidden worry read aloud by the other side.
- Name one shared cost concretely to seed the first list if the room freezes.
