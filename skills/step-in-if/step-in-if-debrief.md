# Step In If… — Debrief

> The exercise does the seeing; the debrief turns the seeing into trust — without it, the circle was just calisthenics.
> Use with `step-in-if-guide.md`. The landing phase at minute 12 is the compressed version; this sheet is the full one.

## How to run this debrief

- Run it while the room still feels the shape of what it saw — five minutes after the last step-back, not the next day.
- Hold the no-explaining rule into the debrief: people may say what it *felt* like, but nobody is asked to confess *which* statement they stepped in to.
- Close on one assumption broken and one conversation worth starting. A debrief that ends in general warmth changes nothing.

## Name what it felt like

- When you stepped into the centre and looked around, what did it feel like to see others already there — or to be the only one?
- Which statement made you surprised at who stood with you? Being surprised is the assumption breaking.
- Was there a statement you didn't step in to but wanted to? What held you back — the room, or the rule that you can't explain why?

## The six debrief questions

**1. The shared surprise.**
"Which step-in made you realize someone you'd written off as 'totally sorted' carries the same thing you do?"
→ Targets: the specific quiet assumption just broken — name it and it stops being private.

**2. Standing alone.**
"What was it like to be the only one in the centre — and what would it take for that to be survivable rather than humiliating here?"
→ Targets: the someone-alone moment; being the only one, seen, is its own kind of trust, but only if the room holds the beat.

**3. The un-stepped step.**
"Which statement did you *not* step in to, even though it was true? What made the room too expensive for it?"
→ Targets: the cost curve of speaking up — the missing steps are the honest safety data.

**4. Reading each other differently.**
"You now know, physically, things about each other nobody said out loud. How does that change how you'll read each other next week?"
→ Targets: converting the sight into default interpretation, not a nice memory.

**5. The value of no-explaining.**
"What's it worth that no one had to explain a step — that it was allowed to just be true? Where else could we let people show, not say, where they are?"
→ Targets: the mechanism itself; this is the trust the exercise actually builds.

**6. The next run.**
"Which quiet, hard-to-say statement should we make safe to talk about properly — and who starts that conversation? When do we run this again, with what statements the moment calls for?"
→ Targets: one named follow-up conversation and a date; belonging shown once goes stale.

## Failure modes of the debrief

| Failure mode | Why it happens | The rescue |
|---|---|---|
| It turns into confessions | People feel they must justify a step to speak at all | Restate the rule out loud: you may say what it felt like, never which statement you answered. The watching is what kills the safety. |
| General warmth, no specifics | The debrief drifts to "that was nice" because naming an assumption costs something | Ask for the one assumption, about yourself, that broke today — the facilitator answers first. |
| The alone-stepper gets smoothed over | The room rushes past the hardest sight to comfort it | Don't over-comfort; hold the beat, thank the honesty, and come back to that statement's topic in question 6. |
