# Step In If… — Facilitator Guide

> Folder: `step-in-if` · Category: trust · 8–20 people · 15 min · offline + online · print props · tool pack
> License: CC BY 4.0

A 15-minute, 8–20 person trust and belonging exercise. The group stands in a circle. A statement is read aloud — "Step in if you've ever felt like the new person who nobody showed the ropes to" — and anyone it's true for steps into the middle, looks around at who's standing with them, and steps back. No explanations, no names attached. It makes shared experience visible in your body, which is faster at building trust than any round of introductions.

**How this relates to the other trust folders:** Step In If… *shows* a team how much it already shares — no ratings, no written record. The Safety Check *measures* how safe the room is on four questions; The Reliability Ledger and The Trust Timeline *repair* what has quietly broken. Run this one when the missing thing is the sight of common ground, not a number or a rupture to fix.

## Objectives

By the end of the activity, participants will have:

1. Physically seen, several times over, how many people in the room silently share an experience they assumed was private.
2. Had at least one quiet assumption about a colleague broken without a word being said about it — including, likely, one about themselves.
3. Named in the debrief one thing they'll now assume less about someone, and one hard-to-say topic worth a proper conversation.

## When to use this activity

- A team (or two teams that must work together) that's civil but hasn't yet seen how much they share.
- A new group where people are performing confidence and hiding that they feel like outsiders.
- You have 8–20 people who can stand in a circle (or raise a hand online) and 15 minutes — and you want belonging shown, not announced.

## Setup

**Clock:** fifteen minutes end to end. Give it twenty if people are genuinely stiff about standing in a circle — the first two statements cost more than the rest.

**Space:** a clear centre everyone can step into and see each other from; standing room for a loose circle. Below 8 it's thin; above 20, run two circles.

**Materials per circle:**

| Item | Source |
|---|---|
| The six card faces — statement, step-in, look-around, step-back, someone-alone, debrief | `step-in-if-prop-cards.pdf` |
| 8–10 "Step in if…" statements, easiest first (printed on the statement card, or written for this team) | `step-in-if-prop-cards.pdf` (rewrite them for your team) |
| 1 × poster with the two rules in view (optional but recommended) | `step-in-if-activity-poster.pdf` (A3) on the wall |

**Grouping:** one circle of 8–20; the whole group runs together, one reader. Remote, each person unmutes or raises a hand instead of stepping.

**Roles:** you (or a rotating reader) read statements aloud and hold the two rules — nobody explains a step, nobody watches for who didn't step; everyone else simply steps in when it's true, or doesn't.

## How to run it

| Time | Phase | Facilitator does | Participants do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Set | People stand in a circle. State the two rules and the one commitment: nobody is asked to explain a step, and nobody is watched for who didn't step. | Get into the circle; agree out loud that the rules hold |
| 3–7 | Ease in | Read the first 3–4 statements, deliberately small and concrete, so stepping in feels cheap. | Anyone a statement is true for steps into the centre, pauses a beat to take in who's there, steps back out |
| 7–12 | Step in | Read 4–6 statements that climb toward the ones that matter, including one or two where someone might stand alone. Never say why you stepped in. | Step in, look around, step back — the seeing is the exercise |
| 12–15 | Land | Run the short debrief: what it felt like to step in, to stand alone, to see others already there. | Offer one sentence each on what the room just showed them |

Then run the full debrief — see `step-in-if-debrief.md`.

## The card

The six faces step the run in order — statement, step-in, look-around, step-back, someone-alone, debrief — so a reader can run the circle off the deck without narrating mechanics. The someone-alone face exists to prepare the room, in advance, for the statement only one person answers to: being the only one, seen, is its own kind of trust. The debrief face keeps the landing from dissolving into chatter.

## Failure modes

1. **No one steps in.** Your first statements are too big or too exposing. Rescue: start deliberately small and physical ("step in if you've had coffee before this meeting") so stepping in feels cheap.
2. **It becomes about who's watching who.** Rescue: restate the rule out loud and immediately — you may only see who *did* step in, never audit who didn't. The watching is what kills the safety.
3. **Someone stands alone and it's rough.** Rescue: don't rush past it or over-comfort it. Hold the beat, let them be seen, and thank the room for the honesty — that's the moment trust is actually made, not the easy statements.

## Variations

- **New team:** open with low-stakes statements ("step in if you moved cities for this job"), build toward the ones that matter.
- **After a hard project:** "step in if you were exhausted but kept going" — the shared load is the point.
- **Remote:** each person unmutes or raises a hand instead of stepping; the look-around becomes the camera.
- **Rotating reader:** hand the statement card to a different person each round — who reads shapes which steps happen.

## Facilitation notes

- Include a statement or two where someone might stand alone, and say beforehand that it's okay — being the only one, seen, is its own kind of trust.
- No one explains a step. You step in or you don't; it's your own, and that's complete.
- The order is the design: easiest first. A room that has stepped three times cheaply will step once expensively.
