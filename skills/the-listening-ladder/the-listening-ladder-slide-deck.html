The three rungs

  • A — Literal: the listener restates the words only. Did the facts land?
  • B — Feeling: add what sits under the words. Right, or over-reach?
  • C — Unspoken: ask one question about what was left unsaid. Then hold the silence.

The third role: the validator

  • The card has two faces only — landed and not yet. There is no "close enough".
  • "Not yet" is the gift: it shows the pair exactly where the loop broke.
  • Roles rotate so everyone is a speaker once across the three rounds.

Round C is not advice.

Ask one question, then let the silence hold.

The speaker fills the gap — that is the hearing.

Three ways it fails, three rescues

  • Round C turns into fixing → hold the rule: one question, then quiet.
  • The validator is polite → only two faces on the card; "not yet" is welcome.
  • A feeling gets stated as fact → always close round B with "have I got that right?"

The measure

  • Before pairing: five short items about your own last week at work.
  • After the debrief: the same five, now about the ladder you just climbed.
  • Score the pair's move, never rank a person's ear — the exercise measures the loop.
WorkshopSkill · The Listening Ladder — Slide Deck · CC BY 4.0

Three questions in the debrief

1. Which rung did your pair climb easiest — and which did they skip?

2. Where did the validator say "not yet", and what changed before it became "landed"?

3. What is the one round-C question you will ask in a real conversation this week?

What actually transfers

  • The skipped rung is the one the room already skips at work — name the cost.
  • One pair, one rung to practise, chosen from their smallest positive delta.
  • Re-listening beats ploughing on — repair is a skill, not a setback.
