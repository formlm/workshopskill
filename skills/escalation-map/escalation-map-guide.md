# Escalation Map — Facilitator Guide

> Folder: `escalation-map` · Category: conflict · 4–12 people · 25 min · offline + online · print props · tool pack
> License: CC BY 4.0

A team plots the arc of a conflict as a heat ladder — from calm, to tension, to blowup, to the point where it was no longer recoverable the same way. At each rung, they name what could have kept the situation one degree cooler, and mark the earliest rung where a small move would have been cheapest. No culprit is written on the map; the map is about the shape of getting hot, not who started it.

## Objectives

By the end of the activity, participants will have:

1. Seen the shape of how the conflict got hot — not who started it.
2. Named, at each rung, a small move that would have kept it one degree cooler.
3. Circled the earliest rung where a cheap move was available, and each taken one move for their own next time.

## When to use this activity

- A dispute between teams or individuals has left the room picking sides, and no one can see how it got this hot.
- You want a shared post-mortem of a conflict that doesn't turn into a blame contest.
- You have 4–12 people and 25 minutes; remote works if the ladder is on a shared board.

## The four rungs

| Rung | What it feels like | Typical move that was available |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Warm | Small irritations, unsaid | Say the small thing before it becomes a stand |
| 2 · Heated | Positions harden; tone shifts | Ask a real question, or take a day before answering |
| 3 · Boiling | Public, sharp, sides chosen | Change the room: move it offline, or bring the shared cost onto the table |
| 4 · Point of no return | Actions have been taken | Repair work only; do not pretend a small move still applies |

## Setup

**Space:** a wall or shared board with the four-rung ladder drawn up it; the team gathered around one map.

**Materials per person:** sticky notes and a pen.

**Materials for the room:** the four rung cards (Warm / Heated / Boiling / Point of no return) and a "one degree cooler" move card from `escalation-map-prop-cards.pdf`.

**Grouping:** 4–12 people who were inside, or adjacent to, the same conflict — one shared map, not two blame maps.

**Roles:** everyone plots events and writes moves; you draw the rungs and hold "no names, only the shape."

## How to run it

| Time | Phase | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Frame | Draw the four rungs as a ladder on the wall. Set the rule: no names, no "who started it" — only the shape. |
| 3–9 | Plot | As a team, place sticky notes on the rungs: what actually happened at each level of heat. Start early; if you can't find anything at rung 1, look harder. |
| 9–15 | One degree cooler | On each rung, write what could have kept the whole thing one rung lower. Be specific and cheap, not heroic. |
| 15–20 | Earliest cheap win | Circle the earliest rung with a real move. That's the intervention point the map is for. |
| 20–25 | Personal bridge | Each person writes one thing they will do at their own earliest rung next time — and shares it out loud in one line. |

## Why the shape, not the culprit

Once a map has a name on it, the room argues about that name. Once it only has rungs and moves, the room argues about which moves were available — a very different, and much more useful, conversation.

## The card

Use the faces from `escalation-map-prop-cards.pdf`: four rung cards (Warm / Heated / Boiling / Point of no return), a "one degree cooler" move card, and a blank to author your own. Keep the "no names, only shape" rule on the card in view — it's the one that keeps the map from becoming a grievance list.

## Failure modes

- **Someone writes names or "they". Pull back to the shape.** "What happened at this rung, not who did it."
- **The team only plots rungs 3–4.** That means they skipped the early, cheap part. Push for rung 1 honestly; the value lives there.
- **Moves come out heroic ("have a big honest talk").** Force cheap: one question, one day's delay, one offline move.

## Variations

- **Remote:** the same ladder on a shared board; it transfers cleanly.
- **On a conflict that just cooled:** run it on a dispute that ended recently — the map will name the rung you're actually sitting on now.
- **Two sides, one map:** if both sides can be in the room, plot one shared ladder rather than two competing blame maps.
- **Shortened (15 min):** plot rungs 1–3 only and circle the earliest cheap move; skip the individual bridge.

## Facilitation notes

- The strongest moment is realising rung 2 had a 30-second, costless move nobody took because they were saving for the rungs below.
- Don't let the map become an excuse ledger — every rung's move should be doable by the person drawing it.
