Two kinds of self-assessment

  • Tests measure traits. They label you — and a label is used once.
  • The Compass measures allocation. It locates you — and a map is useful every month.
  • Not “who am I” but “where do my hours actually go?”

Quadrant 1 — the golden track

  • Strength in service of your vision.
  • Hours compound: every hour makes the next hour more valuable.
  • Healthy share: 40%+. On a first run, most calendars hold 15% — that is information, not a verdict.
WorkshopSkill · The Advantage Compass — Slide Deck · CC BY 4.0

Quadrant 3 — the firefighting track

  • Strength in service of today’s pain. Efficient, praised — worth less every month.
  • Firefighting is a season. A season never re-examined becomes a career.
  • The test question: does this work make next year easier, or harder?
WorkshopSkill · The Advantage Compass — Slide Deck · CC BY 4.0

Quadrants 2 and 4 — the edges

  • Q2, the learning zone: fix a weakness only until it stops blocking the golden track — good enough, then stop.
  • Q4, the burnout trap: costs energy, produces mediocrity, proves nothing. End it, don’t improve it.

The four known distortions

The competence mirageBeing needed feels like being valued. Ask instead: easier next year, or harder?
The humility discountStrengths feel effortless — “anyone could do this.” They could not.
The weakness projectTrain a weakness only until it stops blocking the golden track.
The vision confusionPain is loud, vision is quiet. Read the calendar, not the memory.

Three questions, in order

1. Which single Q1 block is worth protecting first?

2. Which Q3 obligation could be delegated or automated within a month?

3. Which Q4 commitment should be ended, not improved?

One move, one date

  • One concrete change, dated, with a check-back in two weeks.
  • Not a life plan. One move.

Re-run monthly

  • The first re-run’s job is honesty about the baseline, not improvement.
  • The delta between runs is the real score. A compass you consult once is a horoscope.
