# The Advantage Compass — Reading Your Map

> One rule first: the quadrants do not judge you; they locate you. A map that flatters is a map that gets you lost.
> Use with `advantage-compass-guide.md`. Reading takes 10 minutes.

---

## The 60-second read

Before any self-improvement plans, answer three questions in order:

1. **What share of your two weeks landed in Q1 (golden track)?** Under 20% is the norm on a first run — most calendars are hostage to Q3. The number is not a verdict; it is the baseline you are negotiating against.
2. **Which single Q3 block is the biggest hour-thief?** Not the most annoying one — the biggest. Name it in plain words: "the Tuesday report nobody reads", "being the unofficial helpdesk".
3. **Is anything sitting in Q4 by choice?** Pain × weakness work that nobody forced on you is the quietest drain there is. It usually wears a virtue's clothes: "I should be more well-rounded."

---

## Quadrant by quadrant

**Q1 — Vision × Strength: protect, then expand.**
This is the only quadrant where hours compound. Two moves: first protect the existing blocks (put them on the calendar as appointments with yourself, not leftovers), then expand by subtraction — every hour reclaimed from Q3 or Q4 goes here first, not to new obligations.

**Q2 — Vision × Weakness: train to "good enough", then stop.**
The learning zone is real, but it has a spending limit. The limit is *minimum viable competence*: enough to unblock the golden track, no further. A would-be speaker who fears slides learns enough slide craft to not distract — and stops. Mastery here is procrastination in a productivity costume.

**Q3 — Pain × Strength: delegate, automate, or taper.**
The trap wears a salary and a compliment. Three exits, in order of preference: hand it to someone whose Q3 is their Q1 (for them it is the golden track — this is what delegation actually is), automate the repeatable part, or taper your involvement down month by month. What you must not do is get better at it — excellence in Q3 raises the demand.

**Q4 — Pain × Weakness: end it, don't improve it.**
Effort here has negative yield: it costs energy, produces average output, and crowds out Q1 hours. The exit is not training — it is removal. Negotiate it away, trade it, or quit the arrangement that requires it. "But it's my job" is a Q4 answer to a Q1 question: the job description can be renegotiated, your calendar cannot be refunded.

## The two-week audit, quantified

| Quadrant | Your hours | Healthy share | If you are over |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 golden track | ____ | 40%+ | Expand the definition — you may be hiding comfort inside "vision" |
| Q2 learning zone | ____ | 10–15% | Check for gold-plating: training past the point of unblocking |
| Q3 firefighting | ____ | under 30% | Pick the biggest block; run the three exits |
| Q4 burnout trap | ____ | under 5% | Something needs to end, and you already know what |

The healthy shares are not commandments — they are what the calendar of a person with a strategy tends to look like. A quarter spent launching something new legitimately looks different. What is never healthy: Q3 + Q4 above 60%, quarter after quarter.

---

## When the map disagrees with your self-image

The audit says 5% golden track; your self-image says "I'm basically doing what I love." Both are honest — they measure different things. The self-image measures preference; the calendar measures allocation. When they disagree, believe the calendar: allocation is what the next three years will be built from. Preference is what the exit interview will be about.

The reverse distortion also pays a visit: people in a hard Q3 season (a launch, a crisis, a rescue) read their temporary calendar as a permanent character flaw. Seasons end. Re-run the compass after the season, not during it — but do re-run it. "Temporary" that is never re-examined is how Q3 becomes a career.

## The exit moves, as one-liners

- Q1: "This hour makes the next hour more valuable. Protect it like a client meeting."
- Q2: "Learn it until it stops blocking me. Then spend the rest of the budget on Q1."
- Q3: "Being good at this is the trap. Hand it, automate it, or taper it."
- Q4: "Improving this is still spending my best hours on my worst work. End it."

---

## Re-running the compass

Monthly, 25 minutes, same four steps. The point of a re-run is not the score — it is the delta: which quadrant grew, which shrank, and whether the one move from last month actually happened. A compass you consult once is a horoscope.
