# Silent Lineup — Facilitator Guide

> Folder: `silent-lineup` · Category: leadership · 8–20 people · 10 min · offline · optional print · assessment-type
> License: CC BY 4.0

The group lines up by birthday — no talking, no writing, no phones. Ten minutes of pure non-verbal coordination, and a debrief about who led, who followed, and what leadership looks like when the loudest tool is taken away.

---

## Objectives

By the end of the activity, participants will have:

1. Watched **leadership emerge without words** — gestures, position, initiative.
2. Seen their own default: the one who organizes, or the one who waits to be organized.
3. Practiced one coordination move: **make yourself legible** — hold up fingers for your birth month.

---

## When to use this activity

- The room needs to see leadership emerge *before* anyone is appointed — the fastest opener for a session on informal authority.
- You have ten minutes, a stretch of floor, and 8–20 people who will talk over every other exercise.
- As a warm-up before `rotate-the-lead` or `the-decision-rights-map`: this is the quickest way to see who moves first, and who waits.

---

## Setup

**Space:** a clear wall or line on the floor the group can stand along.

**Materials:** none for the lineups themselves — a timing card is not needed; you count the three minutes. The folder adds an optional poker deck (rules, gesture code, observer, timing) for rooms that want the observation made visible.

**Rules — say them out loud:** "Line up by birthday, January on my left, December on my right. No talking, no writing, no phones. You have three minutes. Go."

**Grouping rule:** the whole group at once. Over 16 people, run two lines and compare how each organized itself.

---

## How to run it

| Time | Phase | Facilitator does | Participants do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | Frame | State the rules. "Silence starts now" | Stand, scatter |
| 1–4 | The lineup (3 min) | Time it. Say nothing. Watch for who checks edges (January?) and who helps stragglers | Gesture, shuffle, compare birthdays on fingers |
| 4–5 | Verification | Walk the line, each person says their birthday out loud — once, in place | Errors surface, quick fixes allowed (still silent) |
| 5–8 | Round two: seniority | "Line up by years in the company. Same rules." | New dynamics — this one is harder to gesture |
| 8–10 | Bridge to debrief | "Two lineups, two systems. Hold your memories — the debrief needs them." | Transition |

Then run the debrief — see `silent-lineup-debrief.md`.

## The card

The optional `silent-lineup-prop-cards.pdf` (poker spec, 63.5 × 88.9 mm, bleed and cut guides, branded back, blanks) carries four faces: a **rules** card — the three bans, visible to the whole line; a **gesture code** card — fingers-for-month, the legibility pattern; an **observer** card — for anyone who wants to track who moves first and who unsticks the jam; and a **timing** card — three minutes made physical. Blanks take your own codes. With no cards at all the activity runs; the deck turns the facilitator's private observation into the room's shared one.

---

## Failure modes

1. **Someone talks "just to fix one thing."** Rescue: restart the round with an amused "and again — in silence." The rule is the activity; one word collapses it.
2. **The group clusters and stalls in the middle.** Rescue: no rescue — the stall is data. The debrief question "who could have broken the jam?" is built for this.
3. **Verification reveals a long run of errors.** Rescue: keep it playful — errors are the point of verification, not an embarrassment. Applaud the fix, not the perfection.

---

## Variations

- **Blind lineup:** eyes closed for round two — now touch is the only channel (pre-agree a shoulder-tap alphabet).
- **Order by opinion:** line up from "strongly agree" to "strongly disagree" on a work statement — a silent temperature check that feeds straight into a discussion.
- **Remote:** impossible in the original form — replace with mute-cam ordering: everyone types their birthday into chat simultaneously, then self-sort on a shared whiteboard in silence.

---

## Facilitation notes

- Your silence is the instrument. The moment the facilitator helps, leadership has been artificially supplied and the data is spoiled.
- Debrief in position — walking the line while asking keeps the evidence visible and the memories fresh.
- Run it early in the session. A room that has just lined up silently can handle harder truth talk afterwards; the reverse rarely works.
- If you hand out the optional prop deck, give the observer card *before* round one, not during it — an observer who arrives late misses the emergence, which is the whole point.
