# Yes-And Ramp — Facilitator Guide

> Folder: `yes-and-ramp` · Category: creativity · 4–12 people · 15 min · offline + online · print props · tool pack
> License: CC BY 4.0

A group builds one idea by going around and adding to it — each person must start from what the last person said ("yes, and…") rather than replacing it ("yes, but"). One prompt idea goes up a ramp of accepts-only contributions until the group can't recognise where it started. **Unlike a brainstorm that edits ideas down to the safest one, this ramp only builds up — the mechanic is a muscle: it trains building-on-others over editing-them, which is the difference between a session that stalls and one that compounds.**

## Objectives

By the end of the activity, participants will have:

1. Built one ordinary idea up through a full round of "yes, and" turns — never deleting the last one.
2. Felt where the "that won't work" reflex kicks in, and pushed past it on purpose.
3. Read back how far the seed travelled, and named where that reflex costs them in real reviews.

## When to use this activity

- Your idea meetings collapse into "that won't work" before anything gets built on.
- You want fifteen minutes of pure generative momentum to open a design or naming session.
- You have 4–12 people comfortable speaking in a round; it runs on a call too.

## The one rule

Every turn begins by accepting the previous contribution — out loud, in the words "yes, and" — then adds one thing. No "but," no repeating to steer, no editing someone's idea into your better version. If you can't build on it, build on it anyway.

## Setup

**Clock:** 15 minutes total — seed 1, ramp 7, two loops 2, read back 4, bridge 1.

**Space:** a circle or a call; one board or shared doc that keeps the running ramp visible to everyone.

**Materials per person:**

| Item | Source |
|---|---|
| Nothing but their voice | — |
| A pen (if writing the ramp down) | bring your own |

**Materials for the room:** the seed card, the "yes, and — no but" rule card and blank build cards from `yes-and-ramp-prop-cards.pdf`, and the A3 poster up on the wall.

**Grouping:** 4–12 people comfortable speaking in a round; one idea at a time.

**Roles:** each person accepts the last turn and adds one; you keep the running note, call the rounds, and do the light-touch "yes, AND" reset when someone slips a "but".

## How to run it

| Time | Phase | Facilitator does | Participants do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | Seed | Write a deliberately ordinary prompt on the board ("a coffee shop"). | Read the seed; get ready to build |
| 1–8 | Ramp | Go around; each person says "yes, and…" and adds one turn to the idea. Keep a running note so the ramp is visible. | Take turns: accept the last contribution, add one new thing |
| 8–10 | Two loops | Go around a second and third time so it climbs several stories. | Continue building; push the idea further |
| 10–14 | Read back | Read the whole ramp from the first line. Compare it to the seed — the distance is the point. | Listen; notice how far the seed travelled |
| 14–15 | Bridge | One line each: where your real "that won't work" reflex cost you a ramp like this. | Share one real example of the reflex costing them |

## Why accepts-only

"Yes, but" feels like agreement and is really a deletion — it takes the last idea out. "Yes, and" keeps the floor moving up. The rule sounds playful and is strict; the discipline of building on a bad contribution anyway is exactly what people can't do in a real review.

## The card

Use the faces from `yes-and-ramp-prop-cards.pdf`: a seed card, the "yes, and — no but" rule card, blank build cards and a read-back card. The rule card is the load-bearing one — it's what stops a "but" from masquerading as agreement.

## Failure modes

1. **Someone slips a "but."** The ramp stalls and the room reverts to editing. Rescue: light-touch reset — "yes, AND — go again." Don't shame; the reflex is the lesson.
2. **A person edits instead of adds.** They rephrase the last idea into their better version. Rescue: remind — accept their words first, then your one addition.
3. **The ramp flatlines into jokes.** Every turn is funnier but nothing builds. Rescue: push for one more concrete turn; force it somewhere specific rather than funnier.
4. **The read-back gets skipped.** Time runs out and the group never hears the distance. Rescue: guard the 10–14 block; the read-back *is* the debrief — cut ramp turns before you cut the read-back.

## Variations

- **Remote:** the same ramp in a shared doc, each person adding a line per round; read the whole doc back at the end.
- **Naming / feature ramp:** seed it with a real naming or feature prompt from your work instead of a joke one — the momentum carries into the session.
- **Written round-robin:** each person writes "yes, and…" before the next reads it, so quieter voices land too.
- **Shortened (8 min):** one loop of five turns plus a quick read-back — enough to reset the room's register.

## Facilitation notes

- The strongest moment is the read-back, hearing how far one ordinary seed travelled in ten turns.
- Keep your own additions concrete, not clever — you set the register.
